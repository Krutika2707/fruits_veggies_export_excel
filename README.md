🌍 Behind the Numbers: 2016 Fruit & Veggie Exports

Hey there! Welcome to my data analysis project.

I recently decided to roll up my sleeves and dive into a dataset tracking global fruit and vegetable exports throughout the first half of 2016. My goal was simple: turn a giant spreadsheet of raw transactions into actual, easy-to-understand insights. I used Excel Pivot Tables and Charts to figure out what we're selling, who's buying it, and when they want it the most.

Here is a breakdown of what I found!

📦 What's in the Data?

Everything starts with the main dataset (Export_Data.xlsx - Sheet1.csv). It contains 213 individual orders shipped out in 2016.

For every order, I had the following puzzle pieces:

The "When": The date and month.

The "What": Category (Fruit or Veggie) and the specific product (Apples, Mangoes, Oranges, Carrots, Broccoli, or Beans).

The "How Much": The weight of the shipment in kilograms.

The "Where To": The destination country (USA, UK, Australia, Canada, France, Germany, or New Zealand).

💡 The Big Takeaways

Instead of boring you with endless rows of numbers, here are the coolest things I discovered from the data:

1. Mangoes are the Heavyweights 🥭

When we look at sheer volume, we shipped a massive 1,029,734 Kg of produce in total.

The King of the Cargo: Mangoes completely dominated. We shipped almost 400,000 Kg of them (397,374 Kg to be exact).

Fun Veggie Fact: If you're wondering about the vegetables, carrots held their own, making up 13.30% of our total export weight.

2. People Really Love Apples 🍏

Wait, if mangoes weighed the most, they must be the most popular, right? Not exactly!
While mangoes were shipped in massive, heavy bulk, Apples were actually our most frequently requested item. Out of our 213 total orders, apples were ordered 40 times.

3. The US is Hungry for Fruit 🇺🇸

I wanted to know which countries were our best customers.

The United States is by far our biggest buyer, placing 57 orders in total.

They have a massive sweet tooth: 42 of those 57 orders were just for fruit.

To put that into perspective, the US alone accounts for 28.8% of all fruit orders globally, leaving Australia (13.0%) and New Zealand (8.9%) trailing behind.

4. March is "Mango Madness" Month 📅

Looking at the timeline from January to July, the demand wasn't flat—it spiked depending on the month.

Mango exports went absolutely crazy in March, hitting a massive peak of 94,485 Kg.

Meanwhile, our best month for shipping Apples was a bit earlier, peaking in February at 47,349 Kg.

📂 A Quick Guide to My Files

If you want to poke around the actual data, here is how I organized everything:

Sheet1.csv: The messy, beautiful raw data I started with.

Pivot Table 1.csv: Where I figured out the total weights and percentages (the Mango and Carrot stats).

Pivot Table 2.csv: A simple count of how many times each item was ordered (the Apple stat).

Pivot Table 3.csv: A breakdown of Fruit vs. Veggie orders by country (the USA stat).

Pivot Table 4 & 5.csv: The month-by-month timelines tracking when different items peaked.

Answer for sheet 1.csv: My final "cheat sheet" where all these answers and percentages live.

TL;DR

Mangoes bring in the heavy volume, but Apples get ordered the most often. If you want to make a sale, call the United States—especially if you're selling fruit—and get ready to work overtime shipping mangoes in March!
